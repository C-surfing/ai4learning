# Arc 03 — Paper Reading / Argument Reconstruction

## Mission shape

Learner should become able to reconstruct what a paper claims, why the method is supposed to address the problem, what evidence supports the claim, and where the argument remains limited.

## Target capability

Move from summary to research judgment:

```text
problem → claim → method → evidence → limitation → judgment
```

## S1 — reconstruct before critique

Choose one real paper or section. First establish whether the learner can identify:

- the central research question;
- the main claim;
- the mechanism or method proposed;
- what result would count as evidence.

Do not let the tutor jump directly to critique if the learner cannot yet reconstruct the authors' case.

## S2 — retrieval + mechanism

Without replaying the abstract, ask the learner to reconstruct why the method should work and which assumption is doing the most work.

Distinguish memorized terminology from causal understanding.

## S3 — representation perturbation

If useful, convert prose into one compact representation:

- claim/evidence map;
- method/dataflow diagram;
- ablation → interpretation table;
- assumption → consequence chain.

Then switch back: require the learner to explain the paper from the representation without simply reading labels.

## S4 — transfer / research judgment

Use a nearby paper, alternative explanation, or hypothetical experiment. Ask what evidence would discriminate between competing interpretations.

Transfer is supported when the learner can use the same claim/evidence reasoning on a new paper or experimental design.

## Runtime questions

- Did the tutor summarize instead of teaching argument structure?
- Did source/tooling work overwhelm learner interaction?
- Did the learner generate a judgment or merely accept the tutor's critique?
- Did the representation make dependencies visible or flatten nuance?

## Exit evidence

A strong arc ends with the learner independently mapping a novel claim to evidence and identifying at least one assumption/limitation without being handed the critique.
